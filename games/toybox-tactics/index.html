Toybox Tactics

This is my self-published game. What does that mean?

For this project, we wanted it to be commercial from day 1. That meant going about development quite differently, as we had to not only go through Steamworks to get the game on Steam's marketplace, but also form a proper LLC. That LLC is known as Spark'd Games. It might even get its own website at some point, but for now we just have a LinkTree as our HQ.

This game was created mostly by two people. Benjamin Barnes, as well as myself. We also had an original soundtrack done by Jonah Thomas, and marketing help from Shaina Zaccagnino and Axel Rodriguez-Leon.

We made this game on top of Wooden Wars, which was originally a jam game made in about two weeks. Even shortly after that game concluded, both Benjamin and I agreed that we could expand that game (with just 5 levels) into something much grander. Of course, I was finishing up my last semester of college at the time, and we were both hard at work on our Senior Capstone which would become Tails of the Tower.

On Development

This was business as usual. I already ran Tails of the Tower pretty tightly, and we took what worked there and used it here.

Notably, Kanban for task management and the weekly meetings. Oh, and Godot as our engine of course.

As a developer on this, my domain lied within the gameplay side. Benjamin was responsible for all of the menus and UI/HUD information. I made a majority of the levels and led the development of new units and level mechanics to justify a tightly made set of 50 levels.

This also came with the addition of steam achievements. These were pretty simple to add, but very well worth that little effort as we want to keep the player engaged and generally become an expert on playing our game. Achievements are a great way to encourage that mastery.

Here's a preview of a gameplay screenshot late into development.

The aim of this game is to get your units (the ones with the blue circles) to capture the enemy towers (red). Units automatically navigate the terrain, but you can also order them around to do things better.

Other stuff

You know, you get into game development because you want to make games. For me that's very open ended. I find enjoyment in many of the tasks that span the creativity and problem solving spectrum. I'm a big fan of coding, making systems, making units, balancing stuff, making levels... Essentially everything that can possibly go into the game production pipeline. Besides narrative stuff (though, i've never really given it a shot...).

Well, I wasn't as big of a fan of the marketing side of things. However, to make a successful game it's very important. I made quite a few shorts to promote the game, and had them shared across all of our social media.

And, it turned out I was actually quite a fan of making these shorts after pumping out half a dozen of them. I just wasn't that big on finding new good places to share them, but, that's what my teammates were for.

On the side is one of the shorts I made. The first few were modeled after shorts of games that I would see on Youtube recommended to me, more styled for "Here's some video game dev advice I have". Thankfully, I had a lot of stock development footage to make the production of this short as simple as cutting parts together where I was commentating over the video on a certain topic.

There's a good moral in here about giving everything a try, even if you don't think you're going to be a fan of it. I could see myself just working on creating shorts from provided footage, if needed. Of course I'm much more talented on the development side of game publishing.
